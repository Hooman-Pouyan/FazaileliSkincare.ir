مهدیه فضائلی · Mashhad

Lapis, gold,
and firouzeh.

A design language for fazaieli.ir, sampled from the institute itself rather than invented — then measured, because a palette chosen for mood has to survive contact with contrast requirements.

Where it came from

The building is more elegant than the graphics.

The interiors are restrained and contemporary: bamboo in glass on white pebbles, a deep teal ceiling, gold cove light grazing an indigo wall, fluted green glass, champagne arches. The Instagram post templates — heavy gold frames, blue-to-gold gradients, outlined display type — are the visual language of Iranian beauty marketing in general, and they undersell the room they were shot in.

So the site takes its cue from the architecture. The medallion stays exactly as it is — it is a strong, jewel-like mark — but it sits on quiet ground instead of competing with a gradient.

Not navy. Lapis.

#2D389A measured

Hue 235, high saturation — Persian ultramarine, not corporate navy. With gold and turquoise it is the classic Isfahani triad, and it is already true of the logo.

Not bamboo sage. Teal.

The fluted glass and ceiling measured hue 177. The literal bamboo is a photographic motif — living green against white — not an interface colour.

On cool white · #F7F8F8

Three of these cannot carry text.

On lapis · #161B4A

The rhythm is the design

Long stretches of ink on cool white, punctuated by deep lapis sections where gold and turquoise are allowed to appear. The alternation is derived from the measurements, not imposed on them — and it happens to be exactly how the institute is lit: bright white corridors opening into indigo rooms with gold light along the cornice.

This page is built the same way. You are inside one of the lapis rooms right now.

Typography

Persian is the source text.

English is the translation, not the other way round. Writing English first produces stilted Persian — and Persian is the primary market. Persian body copy sets at 1.8 line-height; Latin does not need as much.

⚠️ No webfont is ever fetched at runtime. From Iranian infrastructure a request to fonts.googleapis.com hangs or fails and takes the stylesheet down with it — a failure found in an audit elsewhere and worth inheriting the lesson from. Every face ships from /public/fonts.

Form

Hairlines, not shadows.

Shadows are what make a site look like a template. Separation comes from whitespace, tone shift, and a one-pixel gold rule — the one place gold is unambiguously right, because it never has to carry a letterform.

NO CARD GRIDContent sits on the ground plane. A product tile is a borderless image with type beneath it — no box, no shadow, no border.
RADII2px on inputs. 16–24px on images and drawers. Nothing in between, so the two never look like a mistake.
ASYMMETRY60/40 and 70/30 splits, echoing the arched niches. Most of what reads as considered rather than corporate.
SPACE96px minimum between sections on desktop. Crowding kills this look, and the space itself is not crowded.
MOTION400–600ms, cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1). Fades and 8–16px rises. No parallax, no bounce, no autoplay.
LOGICAL PROPERTIESmargin-inline-start, never margin-left. The whole layout mirrors between Persian and English with no second stylesheet.

The shell

A rail, not a header.

Removing the horizontal header gives every page its full width for photography — the best asset this brand has — and makes "which room am I in" an ambient fact rather than something inferred from breadcrumbs. On mobile the rail becomes a bottom bar with the same marks. It sits at the inline-start edge: right in Persian, left in English.

One thing that is not a design question

Before and after.

The before/after gallery is the most persuasive content this brand has and the highest-consequence surface on the site: identifiable photographs of clients' faces, attached to a medical-adjacent treatment.

Consent is recorded per case and is revocable. A missing consent row hides the case rather than showing it — default deny, so a data-entry gap fails safe. Revocation is one action in the admin, never a code change. Those pages carry no third-party analytics and no embeds.

Treat it as health data, because that is what it is.
