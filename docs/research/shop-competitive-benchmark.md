# Shop competitive benchmark — practitioner-led and Iranian public-market pass

**Dates:** 2026-08-24–2026-08-25  
**Decision-map ticket:** [`#3`](../15-shop-product-discovery-map.md#3-what-does-the-competitive-market-prove)  
**Purpose:** Extend [`08-competitive-research.md`](../08-competitive-research.md) with two practitioner-led skincare businesses that are closer to Fazaieli's combined Shop, Booking, and Academy model than mass-market beauty retailers.

## Executive read

Face Reality demonstrates the stronger **business system**: one named method connects evidence, at-home products, in-clinic care, a guided quiz, certified experts, professional training, wholesale inventory, marketing support, and lead distribution. Emily Rockwell demonstrates the stronger **local-practice posture**: practitioner authority, paid consultation, transparent service pricing, personalized AM/PM routines, follow-up care, client testimony, and a direct PDP-to-concierge handoff. A later live Iranian-market pass adds a different signal: Khanoumi and Roja Shop make Persian mobile commerce, search, customer-service policies, trust marks, and campaign entry points highly visible, while Torob trains customers to compare availability, seller offers, and price history. Neither model should be copied wholesale. Fazaieli should keep one coherent platform, make Mahdieh's approved method the organizing spine, preserve self-service shopping, and answer Iranian expectations for price clarity, authenticity, support, and mobile purchase recovery without becoming a discount marketplace. The largest strategic opportunity remains the flywheel connecting Academy graduates, verified professional status, product access, ongoing support, referrals, and customer trust.

## Research scope and limits

### Observed directly

Public, rendered desktop journeys were inspected at a reported `1280 × 720` viewport:

- Face Reality: all-products collection, Clear Skin Method, Skin Quiz, kit PDP, active-treatment PDP, Acne Expert Locator, professional certification, and a dated professional class.
- Emily Rockwell: homepage, skincare-regimen process, public price list, external Shopify homepage, all-products collection, product PDP, and the embedded-booking integration shell.
- Khanoumi: public homepage at desktop and `390 × 844`, including search, category and campaign entry, product shelves, cookie choices, support/policy links, trust marks, and mobile bottom navigation.
- Roja Shop: public homepage at desktop and `390 × 844`, including search, navigation, campaign shortcuts, consultation/gift-card entry, support/legal links, support hours, trust marks, and chat shell.
- Torob: a `390 × 844` sunscreen result page, including search, stock/in-person filters, sorting, price-history actions, duplicate-looking result names, and seller-price ranges.

No account was created, no cart or checkout was submitted, no personal information was entered, and the Face Reality quiz was stopped at its email and ZIP-code gate. Iranian-site observations were public-surface research only; they do not establish legal compliance, product authenticity, fulfilment quality, or actual checkout behavior.

### Coverage gap

Face Reality and Emily Rockwell mobile verification was attempted on 2026-08-24, but the earlier inspection surface remained at `1280 × 720`; their mobile layout, touch behavior, performance, and keyboard behavior remain **unverified**. The 2026-08-25 Iranian pass did render distinct `390 × 844` layouts for Khanoumi, Roja Shop, and Torob, but no performance trace, screen-reader audit, checkout, login, or authenticated flow was run. The Emily Rockwell page exposed a Boulevard booking iframe, but the service-selection journey inside it was unavailable. Mootanroo and Rojelab failed TLS negotiation from this environment. These gaps must not be read as passing results.

## Iranian public-market pass — 2026-08-25

### Khanoumi: comprehensive mobile retail, dominated by promotion

**Observed evidence:** The public homepage exposed Persian search across products, categories, and brands; broad category and brand navigation; dated campaign entry; product carousels with current/original price and discount percentage; customer quotes attached to products; editorial articles; FAQ, purchase/payment help, shipping, return, privacy, and support links; phone support; and Iranian trust/industry marks. The page offered both accept and reject controls for cookie rules. At `390 × 844`, it rendered a dedicated category strip, bottom navigation for home/category/cart/account, collapsible support sections, and the same long promotion-led catalogue. A first-visit promotional overlay competed with the page on the mobile capture.

**What this proves for Fazaieli:** Persian mobile navigation, direct search, explicit customer-service policy access, and visible support are market expectations. Current/original price presentation and dated campaigns are familiar patterns when the underlying offer is real and auditable.

**What to reject:** The homepage is extremely long and discount-dense, combines beauty with supplements, appliances, and gold, and lets promotion compete with product comprehension. Fazaieli's first screen should establish practitioner authority and the three customer paths—known product, concern, or guidance—before campaign shelves. Do not reproduce permanent markdown theatre, a broad marketplace taxonomy, or an interruptive first-visit lead/promotion layer.

### Roja Shop: free consultation and support are commercially visible

**Observed evidence:** Roja Shop exposed product/category/brand search, a free-consultation entry, gift cards, organizational sales, blog content, support and store phone numbers, stated support hours, order/purchase guidance, privacy, terms, FAQ, physical-store links, Iranian trust/industry marks, and a support-chat iframe. The `390 × 844` surface used a mobile menu/search and prominent campaign shortcuts for short-expiry goods, discounts, and credit purchase.

**What this proves for Fazaieli:** A human-help route can be a top-level commercial action rather than a footer afterthought. Support hours, terms, order guidance, and physical/institute continuity should be easy to find. Gift cards and credit/instalment entry are visible market mechanics worth separate feasibility research.

**What to reject:** Consultation value was not explained in the inspected navigation, and the mobile campaign vocabulary still puts discount/credit mechanics ahead of suitability. Fazaieli should name the consultation outcome and preserve product/concern context when escalating. Credit purchase remains deferred until provider, margin, refund, and regulatory evidence exists.

### Torob: price transparency is powerful, but seller aggregation magnifies trust risk

**Observed evidence:** A mobile sunscreen search exposed native search, in-person-purchase and in-stock filters, sorting, price-history actions, favourites/popularity actions, and result links labelled with a starting price. The same or nearly identical product names appeared more than once with very different starting prices—for example a CENTELLA sunscreen and a Vichy sunscreen—without enough inspected surface evidence to determine whether the cause was variant, size, seller quality, counterfeit risk, or duplicate catalogue identity.

**What this proves for Fazaieli:** Iranian customers can arrive price-aware and expect stock status, comparability, and price-history context. Canonical product/variant identity and credible authenticity evidence are conversion requirements, not backoffice polish.

**What to reject:** Fazaieli should not compete as a lowest-price seller aggregator or expose unqualified seller offers. A practitioner-led store wins by making the exact product, variant, provenance, suitability, service, and total cost trustworthy. Price comparison is useful only inside one canonical catalogue—variant-to-variant, routine-to-routine, or current versus genuinely historical price.

### Current Iranian evidence boundary

- Khanoumi, Roja Shop, and Torob were reachable and rendered; their public surfaces are evidence of market patterns only.
- Mootanroo and Rojelab failed TLS negotiation from this research environment and remain uncovered.
- No Iranian PDP, cart, checkout, gateway, account, return submission, delivery tracking, or authenticated personalization flow was exercised.
- No observed trust mark, product listing, review, or price was independently validated against eNAMAD, TTAC, a brand owner, a supplier invoice, or a completed order.

## Face Reality — what the site is doing

### 1. The method is the product spine

**Observed evidence:** The [Clear Skin Method](https://facerealityskincare.com/pages/the-face-reality-clear-skin-method) combines four promises on one page: clinically presented results, certified professional care and in-clinic treatments, a personalized at-home product routine, and lifestyle guidance. It states time-bound results, labels before/after images, distinguishes objective and subjective findings, discloses a multi-center 16-week study of 60 participants, and states that individual results vary.

The same method is repeated across navigation, education, expert discovery, products, professional training, testimonials, and certification. It is not an isolated campaign landing page.

**Inference for Fazaieli:** A practitioner-authored protocol should become a first-class content and merchandising object. It can connect concern education, consultation, a staged routine, product kits, treatment aftercare, evidence, and Academy instruction. The name and clinical claims require Mahdieh's approval; the product should not invent a medical protocol from retailer copy.

### 2. Customers are offered two paths: self-service or expert care

**Observed evidence:** Face Reality lets customers buy ordinary kits and products directly, but the [dry/sensitive kit PDP](https://facerealityskincare.com/products/acne-safe-kit-for-dry-or-sensitive-skin) explicitly says it does not contain professional active products that treat acne and directs customers to a certified expert for a personalized treatment plan. The [Acne Expert Locator](https://facerealityskincare.com/pages/acne-expert-locator) searches by ZIP code or address and discloses that listed specialists run independent businesses.

**Inference for Fazaieli:** Product availability should be permission- and confidence-aware:

- normal retail product: price and purchase;
- uncertain fit: guided questions or consultation invitation;
- professional-only product: full education, no public purchase action;
- adverse-reaction or clinically concerning input: stop guidance and escalate.

Fazaieli does not need an external-provider locator at launch because the institute is the provider. A verified graduate directory may become valuable later if Academy graduates are allowed to receive referrals or sell professional products.

### 3. PLP combines merchandising taxonomy with quick conversion

**Observed evidence:** The [all-products collection](https://facerealityskincare.com/collections/all) showed 85 products, quick view, add-to-cart or select-options actions, sorting, and navigation by:

- skin type: Aging Skin, Dry, Normal, Oily, Sensitive;
- product type: Acne Treatment, Back Bar, Cleansers, Enzymes, Eye Care, Hydrators, Marketing, Masks, Moisturizers, Peels, Serums, Sunscreens, Supplements, Toners, Tools, Travel Sizes;
- concern/focus: Acne Rosacea, Aging, Eye Care, Folliculitis, Inflamed Acne, Noninflamed Acne;
- active ingredient: only Hyaluronic Acid was exposed in the rendered filter data during inspection.

Products display category, size, price, kit composition, and direct actions. Curated modules for award winners, acne-safe kits, and best sellers sit below the initial product grid.

**What to adapt:** Quick purchase for unambiguous variants, kit composition, concern and skin-state entry points, curated modules, and visible product counts.

**What to avoid:** Consumer navigation should not casually mix `Back Bar`, `Marketing`, professional tools, and retail skincare. Fazaieli's public, student, professional, and staff catalogues require explicit eligibility rather than one blended taxonomy.

### 4. PDPs explain fit and create an expert escape hatch

**Observed evidence:** The [Sulfur Spot Treatment PDP](https://facerealityskincare.com/products/sulfur-spot-treatment) presents the active percentage in the core description and separates Benefits, Ingredients, Usage Notes, Key Ingredients, consumer reviews, and professional reviews. The kit PDP explains who the routine is for, what it includes, what it deliberately does not do, and where expert guidance starts.

**What to adapt:**

- one-line suitability before long copy;
- explicit `this is for / this is not for` language;
- active concentration when approved and relevant;
- benefits, complete ingredients, usage, warnings, and evidence as distinct content;
- client and professional perspectives kept separate;
- routine and consultation handoffs based on product risk, not generic cross-sell.

**Observed friction:** The kit PDP showed `(66 Reviews)` near the product title while its visible review module said no reviews had been collected. This may be an integration or rendering inconsistency, but either way it weakens trust. Fazaieli should treat review counts, evidence, stock, and price as canonical data with no competing display sources.

### 5. The quiz is commercially useful but should not be copied literally

**Observed evidence:** The [Skin Quiz](https://facerealityskincare.com/pages/skin-quiz) asked three short oiliness, dryness, and sensitivity questions in the tested path, then required email and ZIP code before revealing results. Marketing consent was displayed separately from acceptance of terms and privacy. The rendered answer choices were clickable `div` elements containing paragraphs, without native buttons, roles, or keyboard tab stops in the inspected DOM.

**What to adapt:** A short progressive guide can reduce choice anxiety and produce a routine or consultation recommendation.

**What to improve:**

- show value before requesting contact information;
- use native, keyboard-accessible controls;
- disclose why each question is asked;
- show source-backed reasoning and uncertainty;
- never imply diagnosis;
- allow an anonymous session-only result;
- request account persistence or follow-up consent only after the result;
- keep marketing consent optional and distinct.

The planned AI Shopping Guide can make the conversation more natural, but deterministic eligibility and safety rules must remain authoritative.

### 6. The professional program is a complete growth flywheel

**Observed evidence:** The [Acne Expert Certification](https://facerealityskincare.com/pages/certification-training) sells an $800 program, then offers an $800 opening-order discount on a qualifying $1,200 product order after certification. Its value proposition includes training, products, dedicated support, ready-made marketing, professional recognition, locator visibility, direct-to-client shipping, and expert-only perks. Completion requires professional documents before certification. A separate [dated Zoom class](https://facerealityskincare.com/products/acne-fundamentals) is sold as a non-refundable, licensed-professional-only product with a clear date, time, topic, and course outline.

This creates a loop:

```text
training → certification → opening inventory → supported practice
         → client results → visibility and referrals → repeat product demand
         → advanced education → stronger professional retention
```

**Inference for Fazaieli:** This is the most important transferable strategy in either site. A local version could eventually connect:

- verified Academy completion;
- professional customer-group access;
- graduate starter kits or product credit with explicit economics;
- practitioner-authored protocols and ongoing education;
- approved marketing assets and claim guidance;
- mentorship and support;
- a public certificate check;
- optional graduate directory or referral eligibility;
- professional reorder tools.

It should not be launched until product eligibility, margins, territorial rules, graduate quality standards, and support capacity are explicit.

### 7. Campaign and lifecycle mechanics

**Observed evidence:** Face Reality uses a free-shipping offer for a first consumer order, segmented newsletter signup for Consumer versus Skincare Professional, award-winner and bestseller merchandising, kits, new-arrival badges, travel sizes, product reveals, offers, cart-reminder consent, testimonials, and dated professional education.

**What to adapt carefully:** Segment lifecycle messaging by Customer, Student, and Professional; create genuine kits around an approved routine; use dated Academy events as real urgency; and let awards or evidence support trust where verifiable.

**What to avoid:** On the inspected collection page, a cookie dialog and a large first-order free-shipping popup competed with the catalogue on first arrival. Fazaieli should earn attention before asking for contact data and should never let promotional overlays obscure product discovery.

## Emily Rockwell — what the site is doing

### 1. The practitioner relationship is the primary sales mechanism

**Observed evidence:** The [homepage](https://www.emilyrockwellskin.com/) leads with customized treatment plans, specialized provider training, and a large client-testimonial section. Testimonials name providers and describe listening, education, treatment selection, follow-up routines, and concrete concerns such as melasma, texture, and aging.

The [skincare-regimen page](https://www.emilyrockwellskin.com/skin-regimens) presents a guided sequence: consultation, personalized treatment planning, procedure expectations, progress check-ins, aftercare, and access to questions between visits. It ends with both `Book a Skin Consult` and `Shop ZO Skin Health`.

**Inference for Fazaieli:** Mahdieh and the practitioners should appear as accountable experts throughout the Shop, not only on an About page. Testimonials should be attached to the relevant service, concern, routine, or practitioner and governed by consent and evidence rules.

### 2. Consultation is packaged as a product with clear value

**Observed evidence:** The [public price list](https://www.emilyrockwellskin.com/price-list) shows named-provider consultation fees, requires a new-patient consultation before appointments, and explains what the customer receives: assessment, treatment education, an AM/PM product routine, before/after progress photos, a two-week follow-up call, and complimentary skincare follow-ups. It also publishes treatment prices, deposit consequences, a 48-hour cancellation policy, and a 15-minute late policy.

**Inference for Fazaieli:** If Fazaieli charges for consultation, the page should sell a defined outcome and follow-up service rather than a vague conversation. Whether consultation is paid, credited toward products, or free above a threshold is a business decision for later research; the website should not assume one.

### 3. The product shop is intentionally narrow but weak at discovery

**Observed evidence:** Emily Rockwell's [external shop](https://shop.emilyrockwellskin.com/) foregrounds ZO Skin Health, a featured growth-factor serum, sunscreens, travel sizes, clinic merchandise, and a newsletter. The [all-products collection](https://shop.emilyrockwellskin.com/collections/all) showed 55 products with only Availability and Price filters plus standard sorting. Product cards display names and prices but no visible concern, benefit, skin-state, or routine role.

**What to adapt:** Curated seasonal shelves such as sunscreens, travel formats, and one genuinely featured product can make a small catalogue feel intentional.

**What to reject:** Fazaieli's customers often enter with a concern rather than a known SKU. Availability-and-price filtering is not sufficient, and default Shopify collection architecture would discard the already-approved concern-first strategy.

### 4. PDPs bridge commerce back to care

**Observed evidence:** The [Growth Factor Serum PDP](https://shop.emilyrockwellskin.com/products/growth-factor-serum-1) includes Benefits, How to Use, Ingredients, Shipping, and Return Policy. It then asks `Want a Personalized Skincare Regimen?` and links to the clinic's contact page so the Patient Concierge can schedule an appointment.

**What to adapt:** Every PDP should have a contextual guidance handoff for customers who are unsure, with the handoff destination determined by risk and intent.

**What to improve:** `Contact us and wait for a concierge` is not enough for Fazaieli's target. The handoff should preserve product, concern, locale, and guidance context and offer an explicit next action: continue with the Shopping Guide, request human help, or book a defined consultation.

### 5. Clinic, shop, booking, and account are fragmented

**Observed evidence:** The clinic content uses `emilyrockwellskin.com`, commerce uses `shop.emilyrockwellskin.com`, booking is embedded from Boulevard, and login points to a Boulevard account. Visual typography and navigation also change between the Squarespace clinic and Shopify store.

**Inference for Fazaieli:** The existing decision to keep one Next.js application, shared identity, and separate route-group layouts is correct. Distinct rooms should feel different without making customers cross domains, identities, carts, or unfamiliar navigation systems.

### 6. Campaign and retention mechanics

**Observed evidence:** Emily Rockwell exposes gift cards, clinic merchandise, a travel collection, a sunscreen shelf, featured products, Google-review links, social channels, and newsletter promises covering exclusive offers, early event access, education, treatment updates, and subscriber-only specials.

**What to adapt carefully:** Educational and event-led campaigns fit expert authority better than permanent discounts. Giftable consultation or treatment credit may be worth researching in the Iranian regulatory and operational context. Clinic merchandise is a bonus, not an ecommerce priority.

## Surface-by-surface implications for Fazaieli

### PHP — `/shop`

**Expected**

- Clear routes for `Shop by concern`, `Shop a known product`, and `Get guidance`.
- Visible practitioner authority and authenticity proof before promotional content.
- Curated shelves for approved routines, post-treatment care, seasonal needs, new arrivals, and travel sizes.

**Differentiating**

- A practitioner-authored method connecting education, routines, products, evidence, and Booking.
- A professional/student path that is visible but permission-aware.

**Avoid**

- Immediate newsletter overlays.
- A generic catalogue grid as the Shop's first screen.
- Mixing consumer products, back-bar supplies, tools, and marketing assets in one public taxonomy.

### PLP and search

**Expected**

- Concern, skin state, product type, brand, country of origin, availability, price, and approved ingredient filters.
- Live counts, informative product cards, variant clarity, and quick add only when selection is unambiguous.
- Server-rendered, indexable concern and protocol pages.

**Differentiating**

- Routine phase and post-treatment state as browse axes.
- Eligibility-aware results for public, Student, and Professional users.

**Avoid**

- Facets that produce mostly one-product dead ends.
- Hiding professional eligibility until checkout.
- Standard price-and-availability-only filtering.

### PDP

**Expected**

- One-line benefit and suitability.
- `For / not for`, variant, price, stock, authenticity, origin, delivery, returns, usage, ingredients, warnings, and approved evidence.
- Canonical review, stock, evidence, and price data with no conflicting counts.

**Differentiating**

- Routine role, protocol phase, post-treatment use, and an expert-guidance handoff.
- Separate practitioner-authored notes from customer testimony.
- A visible reason for restricted or on-request purchasing.

### Guidance

**Expected**

- A short anonymous result before lead capture.
- Native accessible controls and progress that does not pressure users.
- Deterministic safety and eligibility rules.

**Differentiating**

- Persian conversational Shopping Guide grounded only in approved content.
- Context-preserving escalation to human support or Booking.
- Saved routine and follow-up for customers who explicitly opt in.

### Academy and professional commerce

**Differentiating, later phase**

- Verified graduate status linked to professional pricing and eligible products.
- Starter inventory or product credit only after unit-economics review.
- Ongoing education, claim guidance, marketing assets, mentorship, and reordering.
- Public certificate verification and, if quality controls support it, graduate discovery/referrals.

## Campaign and gamification implications

### Campaign systems worth testing

- **Education-to-routine:** one concern lesson leads to a safe starter routine or consultation.
- **Treatment aftercare:** a booked service triggers an approved preparation or recovery routine at the correct time.
- **Academy-to-professional:** course completion unlocks verified resources, eligible products, and a carefully costed starter offer.
- **Seasonal utility:** sunscreen, travel, dry-weather barrier care, or event preparation only when the need and inventory are real.
- **Evidence release:** publish a properly consented case, methodology, practitioner explanation, and relevant care path together.
- **Replenishment:** opt-in reminders based on expected usage, followed by a one-tap review rather than an automatic assumption.

### Game mechanics that fit

- Educational completion and routine understanding.
- Customer-controlled progress notes or adherence check-ins.
- Professional continuing-education milestones.
- Contribution or referral recognition with clear rules.

### Game mechanics that do not fit

- Spin-to-win overlays.
- Fake countdowns or false stock pressure.
- Streak loss designed to create anxiety.
- Rewards that encourage unsafe overuse or unnecessary purchasing.
- Permanent points inflation that becomes a hidden discount system.

These are product hypotheses, not validated Iranian demand. They remain inputs to decision-map ticket #14.

## Conclusion

The strongest direction is a synthesis, not a clone: **Face Reality's integrated method and professional flywheel, Emily Rockwell's visible practitioner and follow-up care, and the Iranian market's mobile, price, authenticity, policy, and support expectations—delivered through Fazaieli's existing one-platform architecture without adopting marketplace discount behavior.**
